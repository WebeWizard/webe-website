# Continuity, emergencies, ethics, and accountability

**Adopted policy.** The resident declaration, effective date, and exact adopted revision are recorded in `../config.json`. No emergency delegation, backup arrangement, recovery objective, review cadence, or completed corrective action exists by virtue of this text alone.

**Proposed owner:** City Manager. Records Officer coordinates custody/backups; Clerk links decisions and authority; independent City Auditor reports findings directly to the actual resident.

## Bounded emergency procedure

**Inputs:** an actual incident report, affected assets/people, observed impact, uncertainty, existing organizational incident requirements, and any genuinely adopted emergency delegation.

1. Record observed facts, time, affected scope, evidence references, proposed severity/containment, and the responsible coordinator; protect sensitive incident details.
2. Notify the actual authorized human and use the applicable existing incident process. A city emergency declaration requires the designated real authority; an agent cannot acquire powers by writing “emergency.”
3. Any future emergency delegation must state qualifying triggers/severity, exactly permitted reversible containment, repository/action/resource limits, notification/escalation, an explicit expiry, termination authority, and independent review. Missing, disputed, or expired delegation means pause affected action and seek real human direction.
4. Even an adopted emergency delegation cannot bypass the charter, external obligations, privacy requirements, required independent controls, or prohibitions on self-approval. It cannot grant indefinite extensions, silently spend beyond limits, authorize unrelated delivery, or remove protected merge safeguards.
5. Record each actual authorized action and result. On expiry, revocation, or containment, stop temporary powers, reconcile permissions and obligations, hand back to ordinary procedures, and independently review the incident and necessary corrective actions.

**Pilot:** none of that emergency authority is implemented. Intake may record a report and escalate it; it does not authorize containment. Engineer delivery still requires a recorded `authorize` decision, and urgency does not make a reserved matter routine. Existing human/organizational incident powers are separate from this policy.

**Resulting records:** incident facts, actual authority references if available, action/evidence timeline, expiry/revocation, handback, review, and open obligations. No template incident is a completed record.

## Continuity and recovery

**Inputs:** actual storage/custody map, critical obligations and dependencies, plugin/state versions, access owners, external approval evidence locations, and human-approved recovery needs.

| Planning decision | Required human choice — all TBD |
|---|---|
| Recovery point objective (RPO) | Acceptable data loss by record/service class |
| Recovery time objective (RTO) | Acceptable restoration time and service order |
| Backup design | Destination separate from the working repository/Git history, independent failure/access boundaries, scope, frequency, encryption, retention, and accountable operator |
| Restricted records | Approved controlled-store backup/access and lawful retention; no sensitive copy into Git |
| Exercises | Authorized restoration location, exercise cadence, acceptance evidence, and independent reviewer |

These are unset planning needs, not approved SLAs or promises already fulfilled. Git history, a second branch, a plugin cache, and a claimed “backup complete” are not a tested recovery strategy.

1. Propose protection for local policies/configuration, canonical records/evidence, restricted-store references, pending obligations, and version/provenance information needed to reconstruct the environment. Preserve access to independently controlled authorization evidence; a backup copy of an editable approval claim does not make it authentic.
2. Obtain actual approval for storage, permissions, costs, objectives, and exercises; verify backups and report actual failures. Do not claim protection before an approved backup exists.
3. After interruption or loss, pause unsafe writes, identify the actual damage and trusted restore source, check plugin/state compatibility, and obtain the required real human recovery direction. Restore in the approved isolated location; do not silently migrate or overwrite surviving history.
4. Reconcile restored records against actual external changes, decisions, deployments, resource consumption, revocations, and unfinished work. A restored appointment must not resurrect revoked permissions or stale authority.
5. Validate structure and evidence, exercise access and representative restoration, record measured loss/time and discrepancies, and obtain independent review plus required human acceptance before resuming controlled work.

**Resulting records:** approved recovery plan/objectives, backup evidence, actual restoration/exercise results, reconciliation, exceptions, and owned remediation. Missing backups or untested objectives remain visible risks, not completed tasks.

## Ethics, independence, and appeals

Disclose role conflicts, relevant affiliations/interests, uncertainty, and errors before handling a case or inspecting/auditing it. Recuse from self-review; Manager/Clerk arrange an actually authorized, competent, independent substitute. If none exists, pause and refer to the resident. No simulated appointment resolves a conflict.

The Auditor has a direct reporting route to the resident, appropriate independent access, and no duty to soften findings for the Manager or implementer. This does not grant unrestricted access to private material. The Auditor cannot audit its own actions, rewrite evidence, approve delivery, or unilaterally overturn reserved resident decisions.

Residents may challenge service, access, policy application, conflicts, or findings. The Clerk preserves the appeal and original record; an unconflicted reviewer documents reasons/evidence and actual human resolution. Disagreement or nonresponse does not erase a finding or count as approval.

## Institutional performance and corrective actions

**Inputs:** actual case/status history, baseline commitments (if genuinely adopted), resource sources, inspection findings, incidents/restoration results, and resident feedback. Manager proposes a small, privacy-minimized review set and human-approved cadence (**TBD**).

Track observed response/elapsed time, missed commitments, recurring defects, estimate-to-actual variance, independent inspection outcomes, recovery results, and relevant resident feedback. Label absent measurements and unapproved targets honestly; no invented satisfaction scores, passing exercises, or fulfilled SLAs.

Each corrective action needs a stable reference, finding/cause, actual accountable owner, proposed or authorized due date clearly distinguished, required change, verification criterion, dependencies, and current evidence. The owner performs the change; an independent reviewer verifies the actual result before closure. Keep overdue, ineffective, or disputed actions open and escalate them to the resident; never close merely because an agent says “fixed.”

**Resulting records:** minimized performance review, findings, corrective-action history, independent verification, and any authorized policy/resource changes. Lessons become current policy only through the separate adoption lifecycle, not through an agent editing a retrospective.
